## Rationale

**Product fit** (3.0/30): The product is a browser rendering engine and terminal renderer that allows HTML, CSS, and JavaScript applications to be rendered in the terminal and accessed over SSH. It is positioned as a creative tool for building terminal applications and BBS-style boards using web technologies. There is no evidence that it automates a specific, repeatable SMB workflow end-to-end or acts as an agent within such a workflow.

**Team & execution** (7.5/25): The project appears to be led by a single founder, 'keepamovin,' who describes themselves as a software designer and 'AI nudger.' They have spent eight years on the browser rendering engine component, with most of the code for the terminal renderer written by AI under their guidance. There is no evidence of a broader team, prior startup experience, or relevant domain expertise beyond this technical work.

**Market & timing** (0.0/20): There is no evidence that the product targets SMBs or addresses a workflow common across many SMBs. The described use cases focus on creativity, self-hosting, and nostalgia for early internet experiences, rather than business operations or back-office tasks.

**Traction** (1.5/15): The only traction evidence is a Show HN post with 9 points on Hacker News. There is no mention of users, customers, revenue, or other adoption metrics.

**Differentiation** (3.0/10): The product is differentiated by its technical approach—rendering web technologies in the terminal and enabling SSH access—but there is no evidence of defensible workflow depth, proprietary data, or distribution advantages relevant to SMB workflow automation.

**Risks**: The main risks are lack of product-market fit for SMBs, unclear workflow automation, and absence of evidence for business adoption. The project appears to be an individual technical exploration rather than a focused SMB solution.

## What would change this call

1. Clearer evidence the product owns one named SMB workflow end-to-end (not just assists with it) would raise the score.
2. A more concrete 'why now' (new model capability, regulation, or category shift) or evidence the workflow is common across many SMBs would raise the score.
3. Surfacing stronger team signal — prior startup/exec experience or a clear technical founder — would raise the score.
